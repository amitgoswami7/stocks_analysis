INDIA SMALL/MICRO-CAP 1-YEAR “DO NOT LOSE MONEY” SCREEN
Date: 30 August 2026

IMPORTANT DISCLAIMER
This is a research watchlist, not a guarantee or personalised investment advice. A stock can fall 30–70% even when fundamentals look good. The user's highest-priority rule is capital preservation, so I have NOT treated “could double” as a certainty. I have also rejected several attractive names where one of the hard filters was not adequately satisfied.

HARD FILTERS USED
1. Indian equities only.
2. Target market-cap position: outside India's top 1,000 companies by market cap.
3. Small/micro-cap.
4. Enough turnover to transact approximately Rs 1 lakh in one normal session.
5. Prefer promoter accumulation, low/currently manageable retail ownership, and rising institutional participation.
6. Prefer positive FCF or a credible path to positive FCF.
7. Prefer high ROE/ROCE, reasonable valuation, and improving earnings.
8. Avoid stocks that have already become a crowded narrative or have had a major recent run-up.
9. Weekly/monthly chart must offer a reasonable entry zone; MACD and RSI are explicitly checked.
10. Technology-disruption risk is avoided.

MACRO BACKDROP
India remains one of the fastest-growing major economies, but the near-term environment is not risk-free. A Reuters poll in late August 2026 estimated Q1 FY27 GDP growth at 7.1%, with growth expected to moderate further; oil above $90, geopolitical tensions and a weaker rupee are important risks. Reuters also reported that foreign investors had sold roughly Rs 2.4 trillion of Indian equities during 2026, while domestic SIP flows remained a major stabilising force. This argues for selective small-cap buying rather than indiscriminate small-cap exposure.

Motilal Oswal Private Wealth's August 2026 strategy remained Neutral on equities overall but Overweight on mid- and small-caps, while ETMarkets quoted Green Portfolio's Divam Sharma saying that hidden gems can remain in small caps despite the greater volatility. Therefore the macro regime is supportive of carefully selected small caps, but not supportive of buying weak balance sheets simply because they are cheap.

SOURCE: Reuters, 25–26 Aug 2026; Economic Times/Motilal Oswal, Aug 2026.

STOP-LOSS POLICY
Never widen a stop because the stock falls. If a hard stop is hit, exit or materially reduce. Re-enter only after the original thesis is re-established.

For a Rs 1 lakh position, risk should normally be capped around 0.5–1.0% of total portfolio capital per trade rather than 0.5–1.0% of the position value. Small caps can gap through stops, so limit orders and smaller position sizes are preferable.

PROFIT-TAKING POLICY
A stock doubling is a scenario, not an entitlement. When a position reaches +25–35%, review the thesis. At +50%, consider taking partial profits and moving the stop to protect capital. At +75–100%, do not wait blindly for a perfect top: recover capital and let the remaining position run only while earnings and trend remain favourable.

WHAT WOULD INVALIDATE THE WHOLE LIST
- India enters a severe risk-off / recessionary regime.
- Oil shock remains above ~$100 for an extended period.
- Rupee weakness accelerates materially.
- Domestic liquidity reverses sharply.
- Small-cap index experiences forced deleveraging.
- Any company reports accounting/cash-flow deterioration, promoter pledge, governance issue, or unexplained auditor qualification.
