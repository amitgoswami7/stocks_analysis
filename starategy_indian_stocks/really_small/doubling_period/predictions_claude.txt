0. READ THIS FIRST — ON "SHOULD NOT LOSE MONEY"
================================================================================
There is no combination of ratios, charts or ownership data that can guarantee
a stock will not lose money — least of all a micro-cap. Small/micro-caps in
India routinely move ±30-50% in a year on low liquidity, promoter action,
single-order dependency, or just broad risk-off sentiment. What follows is a
best-effort screen against YOUR stated criteria, with stop losses attached to
every idea so downside is capped by process, not hope. Position-size small
(these should collectively be a minority of a portfolio), use the stop losses,
and treat "double in a year" as a possible upside scenario, not a base case.
I am not a SEBI-registered advisor; this is not personalised investment advice.

1. METHODOLOGY & IMPORTANT CAVEAT ON "OUTSIDE TOP 1000 MARKET CAP"
================================================================================
- SEBI's official buckets only go to "large cap" (rank 1-100) and "mid cap"
  (101-250); everything from rank 251 onward is "small cap" by regulatory
  definition — there is no official "top 1000" cutoff published anywhere.
- I do not have access to a single live, ranked table of all ~2,000+ actively
  traded NSE/BSE companies by market cap, so exact rank numbers (e.g. "#1142")
  could not be verified with certainty for each stock. What I did instead:
    a) Pulled live BSE Smallcap-index RSI/oversold screens (Trendlyne) to get
       today's actual price/RSI for hundreds of small-cap names.
    b) Cross-checked each shortlisted company's market cap on Screener.in
       (India's standard fundamental data source) and on 2-3 other trackers.
    c) Used the well-documented fact that the Nifty Smallcap 250 index (ranks
       ~251-500 by market cap) had a market-cap floor in the ₹4,000-5,000 cr+
       range in 2026, and that Nifty Microcap 250 (~ranks 501-750) sits mostly
       in the ₹1,500-4,000 cr band. Companies below ~₹1,500 cr market cap are
       therefore very likely to be ranked well below #1000 — this is the
       filter I applied. All 5 picks below have market caps of ₹750cr-₹1.65
       thousand cr, i.e. a small fraction of the ~₹5,000cr+ that typically
       marks rank ~500, so they should comfortably sit outside the top 1000.
    d) I flag Renaissance Global and Andhra Paper as the two closest to any
       reasonable "1000" line (₹1,150-1,650 cr) — please re-verify current
       rank on a live terminal (Screener.in "market cap" sort, or your
       broker's screener) before acting, since prices move daily.
- RSI/technical values are LIVE (fetched today, 30 Aug 2026) from Trendlyne's
  BSE Smallcap oversold screener. MACD readings were not independently
  pulled from a live terminal for each stock (no numeric feed available to
  me) — I've given a qualitative read based on price trend/RSI and flagged
  this; please confirm the actual MACD line/signal-line cross on your
  charting platform (TradingView, Kite, Trendlyne) before entry.
- Financial ratios are the latest reported on Screener.in / Tickertape /
  Groww as of late Aug 2026; verify against the company's latest quarterly
  filing before trading, as one more quarter may have reported since.

PICK 1: GANESH BENZOPLAST LTD (NSE/BSE: GANESHBE)

ports + specialty chemicals (sodium benzoate, food preservatives, lubricant
additives) + rail logistics.

Market cap: ~₹830-860 cr (est. rank: well outside top 1000; comparable peers
like Aegis Logistics are ~10x larger)

Liquidity: NSE/BSE listed, daily traded volumes comfortably support a
₹1,00,000 order in a single session (mid-single-digit-lakh shares/day typical
for this name — confirm live depth before placing a market order).

Financials:
- Revenue ₹433 cr, Net profit ₹72.8-73.3 cr (FY26)
- FY26 profit grew ~93% YoY (₹73.3cr vs ₹38.1cr FY25); Q4FY26 net profit
  swung from a loss to ₹15.3cr
- PE ~15.7x, PB ~1.4x — reasonable, not statistically cheap but not stretched
- ROE ~13% (3-yr avg), ROCE reasonable for asset-heavy storage business
- No dividend paid despite profits (cash being reinvested/retained)
- Sales growth has been slow (~9%/5yr) — this is a re-rating/margin-recovery
  story, not a high-growth story

Ownership:
- Promoter holding 39.0% — LOW for an Indian small-cap (most quality
  promoters hold 50-75%); this is a genuine watch-item, not a plus
- A promoter-to-promoter transfer of ~28.6 lakh shares (Rishi/Poonam Pilani
  to Ravi Pilani) was disclosed 26-28 Aug 2026 — this is an intra-family

- Promoter stake has drifted down ~3.8 percentage points over 3 years — a
  mild negative, not a red flag on its own
- FII/DII holding is minimal (~2%) today — genuine institutional white

Technicals: RSI 37.7 (approaching but not deeply oversold), stock down from
₹130 highs to ₹96 over recent weeks/months — has NOT run up, fits "missed by
the market" criterion. Qualitative MACD read: price momentum has been
negative/consolidating in recent weeks consistent with the RSI reading;
confirm actual MACD cross on your charting terminal.

Why it fits the brief: Genuine earnings inflection (profit +93% YoY), cheap
vs. its own history, virtually unknown/uncovered by large brokerages, low
institutional ownership with room to grow, valuation comfort at 15-16x PE
for a business compounding profit.

Key risk: Low promoter holding + a recent large intra-family share transfer
is the single biggest thing to monitor. Also cyclical/other-income-driven
profit (₹35cr of "other income" flagged by Screener.in) means core-business
profit quality needs checking in the next 1-2 quarters.

Suggested stop loss: ₹78 (roughly 19% below CMP, below the recent multi-month
base and 52-week low support zone). Trim/exit if promoter holding falls
further in the next shareholding disclosure.

PICK 2: XCHANGING SOLUTIONS LTD (NSE/BSE: XCHANGING)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Business: IT services (insurance BPO software, banking BPO, F&A, asset-mgmt
tech) — India, US, Singapore. Majority-owned by a UK-based promoter group
(Xchanging Technology Services).

Market cap: ~₹720-760 cr (est. rank: well outside top 1000)
CMP (30 Aug 2026): ₹90.8 | Stock is down ~26-32% over the past year
Liquidity: Traded on both NSE/BSE; daily value traded is modest for a
micro-cap but has generally been enough for a ₹1,00,000 single-session
order — check live order-book depth on the day, this is the most
liquidity-constrained of the five picks.

Financials — the standout feature is the balance sheet:
- Revenue ₹203 cr (FY26), Net profit ₹59.4 cr — net margin ~29%, unusually
  high for an IT services firm this size
- FY26 profit grew ~20% YoY (₹59.5cr vs ₹49.6cr)
- Cash & short-term investments of ~₹300 cr against a ~₹720-760cr market
  cap — nearly 40% of the market cap is sitting in cash/investments,
  materially cushioning downside
- Debt/equity only ~24%, low leverage
- PE ~12-13x (cheap for a 29% net-margin business), dividend yield ~3%
- Weak point: 5-year revenue CAGR is roughly flat to slightly negative — this
  is a value/cash-compounding story, not a growth story

Ownership:
- Promoter holding 75% — high and stable, zero pledging (clean)
- DII holding: 0%. FII holding: ~0.1%. This is about as close to "zero
  institutional ownership" as you'll find on the NSE — exactly the kind of
  name institutions haven't discovered yet
- Public/retail holding ~24.6% — moderate, not extreme

Technicals: RSI 30.2 today — right at the oversold threshold. Stock has
fallen from a 52-week high near ₹105 to ₹63-91 in recent months — has
clearly NOT run up and is deep in "missing from the narrative" territory.
Confirm MACD on your charting platform; price action suggests a
multi-month downtrend that may be exhausting itself near current levels.

Why it fits the brief: This is the cleanest "hidden gem" of the five —
cash-rich, debt-light, high-margin, zero institutional ownership, cheap
on PE, beaten down on price with no obvious company-specific bad news
found in this search. The catalyst for doubling would be either a
re-rating on discovery (DIIs/smallcase screens starting to notice the
cash pile) or a special dividend/buyback given the cash hoard.

Key risk: Flat/declining revenue — if growth doesn't return, the stock
can stay "cheap and ignored" for a long time (a classic value trap risk).
Also thin free float and light trading volumes mean price can gap on low
volume news.

Suggested stop loss: ₹72 (about 21% below CMP, below the recent 52-week
low zone of ₹63-65 gives room, but ₹72 keeps risk tighter given how thin
the stock trades — tighten further if you see volume/price break down).

Sources: screener.in/company/XCHANGING, hdfcsky.com, simplywall.st

PICK 3: OM INFRA LTD (formerly Om Metals Infraprojects; NSE: OMINFRAL)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Business: Diversified EPC/infra conglomerate — hydro-mechanical equipment
(gates, penstocks) for dams, hydro-power EPC, steel fabrication, plus real
estate/hotel/entertainment side businesses. Long track record on marquee
government hydro projects (Koldam, Teesta, Parbati, Chamera, Upper
Krishna, Almatti, Gosikhurd, Prakasam Barrage).

Market cap: ~₹800-1,240 cr depending on date of quote (very volatile stock;
est. rank: outside top 1000)
CMP (30 Aug 2026): ₹122.8 | 52-week range: ₹71.5 - ₹186
Liquidity: Sufficient for a ₹1,00,000 single-session order on most days;
this is a retail-favourite name with decent turnover, but it is also one
of the more volatile stocks here — check spreads before a market order.

Financials — genuinely mixed:
- Revenue CAGR ~29% (5yr) — one of the strongest growth rates in this list
- Profit CAGR ~18.5% (5yr) — also strong
- BUT: ROE only ~2.7-4.1% (3-yr avg), ROCE ~4.6-13% depending on the
  snapshot/period used by different data providers — capital efficiency is
  genuinely weak and inconsistent across sources, a real flag
- High debtor days (226 days) and contingent liabilities of ₹254 cr —
  typical of a government-EPC business, but worth knowing
- PE is elevated (39-55x across different snapshots) which is NOT cheap for
  the ROE profile — this stock is priced more on order-book optimism than
  on current earnings quality
- Recent large order wins: ~₹200 cr (2,880 MW Dibang hydro project, EPC
  scope) and ~₹129 cr water project — order momentum is a real positive
  catalyst if execution follows through

Ownership:
- Promoter holding 67.05% — healthy and majority-controlled
- Promoter stake has drifted down ~4.6 percentage points over 3 years —
  worth monitoring, though not a large move
- Public/DII participation data was not clearly available in this search;
  verify current DII trend on Screener.in "Investors" tab before buying

Technicals: RSI 34.7 today, stock trading well below its 52-week high of
₹186 (currently ₹95-123 depending on exact date) — has clearly NOT been in
favour recently, giving valuation-comfort-on-price even though PE looks
rich on trailing earnings.

Why it fits the brief: Strong revenue/order momentum in India's hydro-power
and water-infrastructure capex cycle (a genuine macro tailwind — India is
accelerating hydro and river-linking/water projects), share price well off
highs, small enough to be off most institutional radars.

Key risk: This is the weakest-quality balance sheet of the five on pure
return-on-capital metrics, and the PE is not cheap. This is a bet on order-
book execution and macro tailwinds converting into margin improvement — if
execution disappoints, there's little valuation cushion. Treat as the
highest-risk, highest-optionality pick of the five.

Suggested stop loss: ₹98 (about 20% below CMP, below the recent multi-
month trading range floor). Given the volatility here, consider a tighter
mental stop and smaller position size than the other four picks.

Sources: icicidirect.com, screener.in/company/OMINFRAL, tickertape.in,

PICK 4: ANDHRA PAPER LTD (NSE/BSE: ANDHRAPAP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Business: Writing/printing/cut-size paper and pulp manufacturer (Andhra
Pradesh mills), part of the Bangur/International Paper–linked promoter
group. Established, decades-old business — not a speculative micro-cap
in terms of operating history.

Market cap: ~₹1,150-1,240 cr (est. rank: likely outside top 1000, but this
is the closest of the five to any reasonable "1000" cutoff — re-verify
rank before acting)
CMP (30 Aug 2026): ₹77.4 | Stock down ~20-27% over the past year, near
multi-year lows
Liquidity: Comfortably supports a ₹1,00,000 single-session order.

Financials:
- Revenue ₹1,701-1,702 cr (FY26), up ~10.4% YoY
- Net profit ₹18.6-27.6 cr depending on the exact period/snapshot — profit
  has compressed sharply from a much stronger FY23-24 (paper/pulp prices
  were far more favourable then; as of Feb 2025 ROE was ~24% and ROCE
  ~31% over the prior 3 years — those numbers have since fallen hard)
- Current ROE just ~6% (3-yr trailing average, reflecting the recent
  downturn), PE ~18x on depressed earnings
- Company is virtually debt-free historically, with strong interest
  coverage and a healthy current ratio — balance sheet quality is good
  even though profitability has cooled
- FY26 Q4: profit actually declined slightly QoQ even as revenue grew,
  showing margin pressure (raw material/pulp costs, competitive pricing)
  is the real story here, not a demand problem

Ownership:
- Promoter holding 72.4% — high, stable, zero pledging
- This is the highest and cleanest promoter holding of the five picks

Technicals: RSI 38.2 today; stock has clearly NOT been in favour over the
last 6-12 months (down 20%+ and trading near multi-year lows around ₹65-80
versus a much higher base a couple of years ago) — strong fit for the
"valuation comfort, out of favour" criterion.

Why it fits the brief: This is the classic cyclical-recovery/value play —
strong, clean balance sheet, high promoter skin-in-the-game, beaten down
hard on a cyclical (not structural) profit dip, in a sector (paper/
packaging) that has genuine long-term demand tailwinds from e-commerce
packaging and import-substitution. If pulp/paper realizations recover even
modestly, earnings (and the stock) could re-rate sharply given how
depressed the current profit base is.

Key risk: Paper is a genuinely cyclical, commodity-like business — margins
depend on pulp costs and Chinese/global paper dumping, both outside
management's control. A "double" here needs the cycle to turn, which is a
macro call, not a company-specific one.

Suggested stop loss: ₹65 (about 16% below CMP, just under the recent
52-week low zone — if this breaks, the cyclical thesis is likely wrong for
now).

Sources: screener.in/company/ANDHRAPAP, tickertape.in, moneyworks4me.com,

4. MACRO BACKDROP (as context, not as a timing signal)
================================================================================
- India small/midcap segments underperformed large caps for much of the
  past year (BSE Smallcap index materially lagged Sensex in the period
  referenced by search results), which is consistent with several of
  these names being "out of favour" rather than newly discovered.
- A large and growing number of Indian small/microcaps are showing RSI
  readings in the 25-40 oversold-to-neutral band as of this week (30 Aug
  2026), suggesting broad-based small-cap weakness/consolidation rather
  than stock-specific distress for most names on this list — this cuts
  both ways: it can mean a sector-wide bounce is due, or that small-caps
  remain out of favour with FIIs/DIIs for a while longer amid global
  risk-off conditions. Track FII/DII flow data and RBI rate policy over
  the next few months, as a rate-cut cycle or FII inflow resumption would
  likely help exactly this kind of beaten-down small-cap basket.
- Sector tailwinds referenced above: India's hydro/water-infra capex push
  (helps Om Infra), e-commerce-driven packaging/paper demand and anti-
  dumping duty environment (helps Andhra Paper), and continued US/global
  demand for Indian gems & jewellery exports (helps Renaissance Global) —
  these are directional tailwinds, not certainties.

5. HOW TO USE THIS LIST
================================================================================
1. Re-verify CMP, market cap, and shareholding pattern on a live terminal
   (Screener.in, Trendlyne, or your broker's app) the day you plan to act —
   several of these figures moved even within the sources pulled today.
2. Confirm actual MACD line/signal on a live chart — I gave RSI directly
   from a live pull, but MACD needs your charting platform for precision.
3. Place the stop losses given (or your own, tighter ones) as GTT/limit
   orders where your broker supports it, so a bad week doesn't turn into
   a bad year.
4. Size each position small — this is a basket of speculative, low-
   institutional-coverage micro-caps by design (that's what "hidden gem,
   not yet discovered" means), and several carry real balance-sheet or
   capital-efficiency flags noted above. No single name here should be a
   large fraction of a portfolio.
5. Re-check quarterly results (Q2 FY27, due ~Oct-Nov 2026) before holding
   through earnings, since a miss on any of these thin-coverage names can
   move the stock sharply on low volume.
